## What the measurements support

In stationary windows 29–35, each five-second summary contains ~97–101 slow frames. This matches **20 client ticks per second**, not a random Vulkan fence or present hitch. The `client_tick` p95 is typically ~23–30 ms and maxima ~26–37 ms, while non-tick frames often finish in ~4 ms. `Minecraft.runTick()` calls `Minecraft.tick()` at the fixed client tick cadence; VulkanMod's broad hook times that complete synchronous call. Its contents include vanilla client world/entity/GUI work, packet integration, and mod callbacks. This capture cannot yet assign the 20–30 ms within that call to one of those sources. The integrated server runs on a separate thread, so a server overload warning at 03:40:06 does not by itself explain render-thread tick cost. Chunk packet processing or main-thread tasks remain plausible during population.

`game_render` is a second, smaller recurring cost (~4.2 ms/frame in the best slice). `terrain_setup` is ~1.1 ms inside it; uploads are ~0.06 ms. The rest spans world passes, entities/block entities, particles, GUI and mod render callbacks. Existing hooks do not distinguish them. This is why the new profiling commit separates `world_render` from the rest of `game_render`; it does not claim a renderer speedup.

`frame_fence_wait` stays near ~0.002–0.003 ms average in the late windows; `submit_render` and `display_update` each stay around ~0.06 ms. `frame_limit` is zero in-world but is the intentional ~16.7 ms menu cap. The occasional much longer menu limiter maximum can include JVM scheduling/GC wall time. The `unaccounted` bucket is essentially closed in normal world frames. `terrain_setup` and `terrain_uploads` are nested within `game_render`, so adding them to top-level stage averages double-counts. JVM GC deltas in late windows are ~5–18 ms per five seconds and cannot explain ~100 slow tick frames per window. The profiler reports CPU wall time, with no Vulkan GPU timestamps; the low submission/fence numbers do not prove zero GPU cost, especially at this small framebuffer.

## Terrain churn and source-level failure cycle

During windows 29–35, visible nonempty sections remain at 2220, but accepted terrain builds rise **7279→8248**, staging rejections **2035→2994**, and the `sc` count **7911→10083**. The staging store is pinned at **2048/2048 section entries** and ~22 MiB/32 MiB, so the **entry cap**, not its byte cap, is exhausted. Counts are cumulative; `sc` includes copy activity and is not one-to-one with rebuilds. Some newly integrated or legitimately dirtied sections can still account for builds after the view stops moving. The section plateau alone cannot prove that every build is redundant.

There is, however, a directly observed self-inflicted cycle. `ChunkTask.BuildTask.compile` may omit CPU model tessellation for a GPU-owned REPLACE/APPEND section before publication. `ChunkArea.publishVoxels` then calls `RegionVoxelStore.put`; a full global store rejects a new snapshot. Its failure path invalidates GPU inputs and `GpuTerrainSectionMesherBridge.recoverCpuFallback` requests a second build of the same generation's section with complete CPU geometry. Runtime diagnostic summaries show `input_publish/voxel_snapshot_store_rejected` climbing from 127 at 03:40:42 to 1677 at 03:41:55, with `recovery/cpu_rebuild_requested` tracking closely (1684 by 03:41:55). Thus a substantial fraction of continued build work is demonstrably GPU bypass followed by mandatory recovery, though the capture cannot quantify its exact main-thread cost. The initial output reservation failure and a few lighting/voxel GPU upload failures are separate, much less frequent cases.

The one-time Distant Horizons log says its OpenGL LOD draw/fade passes are suppressed under Vulkan while its data/maintenance remains active. There is no repeated DH warning temporally matching the 20 Hz tick cadence. Create/Flywheel and model/resource warnings are concentrated in startup/reload. The SoundEngine initialization failure at 03:39:02 is real but precedes world entry; the numerous JEI/resource/model warnings do not explain the later recurring tick frames. The initial integrated-server `Can't keep up` warning belongs to world entry, not every late stationary interval.

## Change and remaining question

Commits `ac9aa125` and `477bb9d5` check whether the bounded voxel store could accept the captured section **before** the worker enables GPU CPU-bypass. When full, the worker builds normal CPU geometry on the first pass. Replacements of an already resident section remain eligible if their replacement fits. This is advisory because several workers can pass the check concurrently; publication retains the authoritative rejection and CPU recovery path. A bounded-budget smoke checks new-entry rejection, same-slot replacement, byte cap, and released capacity. This is a correctness-preserving reduction of the identified recovery cycle, not yet a measured FPS improvement.

The same commit adds nested `client_level_tick`, `client_entities_tick`, `client_renderer_tick`, `client_connection_tick`, and `world_render` stages, plus `client_tick_other`, `game_render_other`, and `world_render_other` derived averages. `Terrain scheduling: dirtyNotices/scheduled` cumulative counters help separate external section dirties from initial or recovery builds. The new nested timings are not subtracted again from top-level `unaccounted`. The next capture must show whether client tick time is level/entity simulation, packet integration, renderer tick, or the residual mod/GUI/task path; and whether the saturation gate reduces recovery/build churn. A new capture with the same 854×480 window is useful for this *diagnostic A/B*, while the formal baseline still requires a stable near-2560×1440 framebuffer, fixed world/options, and a 60-second stationary settling period.
